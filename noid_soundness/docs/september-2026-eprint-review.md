# September 2026 ePrint review against Parano1d

## Result and scope

The target is Parano1d source commit `69d068f2de8475cd9526474d321c52e1da41d863` and the mainnet v2 bank at activation height 210537. The papers and reviewed PDF hashes are recorded below.

The strongest new lead is the CICO-2 resultant algorithm of ePrint 2026/1905. Its formal expression is lower than the earlier feed-forward projection, but the published theory and experiments do not establish the method for Parano1d's binary feed-forward equations. The Fiat-Shamir compiler remains an explicit assumption in the certificate. The mainnet v2 calculator reproduced the saved result byte for byte.

The exact target is `GF(2^128)`, width four, rate two, `x^7`, eight full rounds, 58 partial rounds, the production round constants and linear matrices, and the actual domain-separated sponge and two-lane feed-forward Merkle compression. The C1 algebraic challenge support is `GF(2^256)` excluding the base subfield. These are separate objects: an attack on an unconstrained permutation, a prime-field Poseidon instance or an ideal random permutation does not automatically attack an accepted recursive terminal.

## 1. Bivariate resultants and the production compression

[Bak and Hostettler, ePrint 2026/1905](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1905) gives a heuristic Villard-style bivariate resultant algorithm for Poseidon CICO-2. Its paper analyzes output equations `P(X,Y)=Q(X,Y)=0` after fixing the other input lanes. For the no-skip width-four round schedule, the formal polynomial degree is `delta=7^(8+58)=7^66` and the paper's ideal-degree bound is `D_I<=7^(2*8+58)=7^74`. Its `omega=2` soft-O monomial is therefore `D_I*sqrt(delta)=7^107`, with descriptive `log2` value `300.386976660164`. The executable certificate now calculates these exact integers and labels the result as **screening only**. Hidden soft-O factors are not included.

The paper's Theorem 4 assumes `char(F)>D_I` to guarantee generic-coordinate stability; production has characteristic two. Its two additional heuristics and Conjecture 2 were experimentally studied on other instances, not the production constants. The full-round experimental comparison is for width eight over prime fields, while the reduced-round width-four experiment is also over a prime field. Production's fixed tagged feed-forward equations contain the first two input lanes in the output and are not the paper's zero-output CICO-2 equations. The formal degree `7^66` also exceeds the field's `2^128` elements, so finite-field polynomial reduction needs separate treatment. These gaps prevent promoting `7^107` to a demonstrated attack cost on Parano1d or using it to calculate a new fixed-Poseidon2b delta.

The previous [ePrint 2026/306 specialization](poseidon2b-august-2026.md) gives `log2(d_I^2)=409.873818620410` for its own feed-forward Gröbner model. That number does not cover the new resultant algorithm. A future proof or experiment that bridges characteristic two, the exact fixed equations and a reachable collision would require reopening the classical audit and potentially the certificate. [ePrint 2026/1969](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1969) describes a second resultant solver; its fast determinant model explicitly does not apply to binary extension fields and it does not provide a production feed-forward attack. The PDF hashes reviewed here are `e77758ca8849db899d1c94f64c1f945a085342e04fc3ea9271be69601723d6e0` and `cfa2e38b7774c32ed7c36f5a7e5058be037e0a22b2deff297316c21888bfafc3` respectively.

## 2. Matrix-selection attacks and parameter provenance

[Jeong, Leip and Son, ePrint 2026/1901](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1901) demonstrates attacks when a designer or supplier can choose or substitute a linear matrix after choosing a target behavior. This is a parameter-provenance problem, not evidence that the public production matrix has a backdoor. We compared all 264 production round constants, 16 external-matrix entries and 16 internal-matrix entries in source order against the Poseidon2b authors' [`poseidon2b_x7_128_512.rs`](https://github.com/Poseidon-Hash/Poseidon2b/blob/7072a9438cf48f1b38ad0092cda5452626914068/binius_poseidon2b/crates/circuits/src/hades/poseidon2b_x7_128_512.rs). All 296 values matched exactly. The pinned reference file has SHA-256 `6a32a107ab40974d259aa0bdd9a11df62c7f42ecd44f48d905b614d0b958fa88`; the canonical encoding of its 296 row-major `u128` values as little-endian bytes has SHA-256 `f7b7ea4bae01d0a62c1d51372046325b4781c641b0ce395ced5ab16c510e6e9b`. A release regression test now checks that value digest against production constants. This establishes correspondence with the authors' reference, not a proof that no weakness exists in that reference.

The revised [Slipway, ePrint 2026/1579](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1579) likewise chooses a matrix as a function of round constants. We found no fixed-instance witness in the revised paper. The reviewed PDF hashes are `676801641309a7e474ce507ae5c3fa2a75c480f5917ddd1a2d7b7526eb435647` for 2026/1901 and `e1878008ef0b993d922eee16ef1b8a949d741869af0dc2ccec2c507bd6de00ad` for 2026/1579.

## 3. Fiat-Shamir inside recursion

[How to instantiate Fiat-Shamir Provably and Practically?, ePrint 2026/2114](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2114) discusses diagonalization against public Fiat-Shamir challenges and proposes challenges from an external secret-key VRF. Its threat model deserves attention because Parano1d's recursive relation verifies earlier proofs and therefore executes verifier logic, including hash operations. The production `FsChallenger` and `Poseidon2bChannel` frame operations and separate domains, while the HistoryStep bank pins class shapes and verifier-key transcript digests. The bounded contract program is interpreted inside a fixed relation; it does not choose an arbitrary new proving circuit. Those checks constrain the attack surface, but they are not a proof that every KRS-style self-reference is impossible in the concrete recursive relation.

The paper's VRF construction needs a secret evaluation key hidden from the prover and a noncolluding evaluator. It is not a permissionless drop-in replacement for the present public transcript. The authors also state that their formal diagonalization analysis covers three-round Sigma protocols and does not yet prove immunity for multi-round GKR. We therefore retain the existing ideal-compiler and fixed-hash premises, record this as an open concrete-compiler review, and make no protocol change from the paper alone. No forged Parano1d proof, maliciously selectable circuit or production transcript mismatch is provided by the paper. The reviewed PDF SHA-256 is `37130eefc34e47767a64b24da71faa99e49771c083a1f8b7998ecf81780183eb`.

## 4. Quantum permutation and Reed-Solomon bounds

[Kim, ePrint 2026/2135](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2135) proves compressed-permutation-oracle error at most `4q/sqrt(N)` for a uniformly random permutation on `N` points, and derives tight sponge and Davies-Meyer query bounds in that model. Production Poseidon2b is one public fixed permutation with tagged sponge and feed-forward uses. No reduction in the paper replaces the event-specific fixed-Poseidon2b `Delta_P2b` premise in our end-to-end theorem. We do not substitute the ideal-permutation bound into the production calculator. The reviewed PDF SHA-256 is `ee5dfbf47e2c93a49cdb2c9d37926ed5a25ba490a7c3cab1fbcc2ad49a184187`.

[Dao, Kominers and Thaler, ePrint 2026/2056](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2056) improves the asymptotic Johnson-regime mutual-correlated-agreement error in every characteristic from a fifth-power to a third-power dependence on the agreement gap. Its beyond-Johnson results require sufficiently large characteristic, which excludes the binary fields used here. The paper does not supply a ready substitution for every finite constant, folded layout and typed root in the current Parano1d calculation. Consequently no query count or soundness value was changed. Re-running a finite-parameter specialization is a separate research task; an asymptotic improvement alone cannot justify reducing a consensus query count. The reviewed PDF SHA-256 is `b67c188ec477b6063caf9c1c06b214c71e358ff09b9517adcdb212ea2700a`.
